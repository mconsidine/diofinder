TeleTrack AUX cable

A Raspberry Pi Zero 2W on the TeleTrack's 6-pin AUX jack. The cable passes through a small interface board with one SN74LVC2G07 and six resistors. The pinout is confirmed by two Synta schematics: the accessory-side "AUX INTERFACE" sheet and the mount's own "Pannel of Synta Multi-Function Mount" sheet. They number the contacts from opposite faces, plug and jack, but describe the same wiring. Check it with a meter before connecting.

Before anything touches the Pi
  • AUX pin 4 is +12 V. It must not be connected to anything. 12 V on any Pi pin destroys the Pi.
  • Check with a meter, mount on, against the power-jack sleeve:
    • pin 4 reads 8–16 V;
    • pin 2 shows continuity to GND with the power off;
    • pins 1, 3 and 5 sit near 5 V.
    Earlier hardware revisions used a different AUX pinout.
  • Ready-made 6-wire telephone cables are often reversed (pin 1 at one end is pin 6 at the other). Check continuity end to end, or use a screw-terminal breakout at the mount.
  • Looking into the mount's AUX jack with the latch slot at the bottom, the contacts from left to right are: PDL, data, +12 V, data, GND, DROP. The panel schematic numbers these 1–6 left to right. This page numbers them from the plug side, so DROP is plug pin 1.
  • Inside the mount the two data contacts are the same wire (TX and RX joined), so the bus really is single-wire.

How it works. The Pi's TX (pin 8) and its Drop output (GPIO17, pin 11) each drive one open-drain buffer channel: a low input pulls the mount's line low, and a high input lets it float up to 5 V through R3/R4 and the mount's own pull-ups. So nothing at 5 V ever reaches a Pi pin, and the Pi never pushes the bus high. R1/R2 hold the buffer inputs high while the Pi boots, so the mount sees an idle bus.

The mount's data back to the Pi (AUX pin 5) passes through the R5/R6 divider: 5 V × 20/30 = 3.3 V. With 18k for R6 it is 3.2 V. Don't use 22k: it gives 3.4 V.

Grey pads 1, 3, 5 and 6 are already used by the BNO055 IMU; leave them alone. Pin 13 (GPIO27) is only for the pin-scan step and isn't part of the finished cable. The jumper colors are suggestions; the board-to-plug colors depend on your flat cable, so identify each conductor by continuity.

  1. Meter the AUX jack as in the caution box above. Stop if pin 4 isn't the 12 V pin.
  2. Build the board. With nothing attached, check:
    • no continuity between the Pi 5V pad and any Pi signal pad;
    • the AUX pin 4 conductor is isolated.
  3. Listen first. Connect GND and the AUX pin 5 path only, then run tt_discover.py --listen 30 with the hand controller plugged in.
  4. Then talk. Unplug the hand controller, connect the rest, and run tt_discover.py --talk --drop 17.
