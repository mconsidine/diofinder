TeleTrack AUX cable

A Raspberry Pi Zero 2W on the TeleTrack's 6-pin AUX jack. The cable passes through a small interface board with one SN74LVC2G07 and six resistors. The pinout is confirmed by two Synta schematics: the accessory-side "AUX INTERFACE" sheet and the mount's own "Pannel of Synta Multi-Function Mount" sheet. They number the contacts from opposite faces, plug and jack, but describe the same wiring. Check it with a meter before connecting.

Before anything touches the Pi
  • AUX pin 4 is +12 V. It must not be connected to anything. 12 V on any Pi pin destroys the Pi.
  • Check with a meter, mount on, against the power-jack sleeve:
    • pin 4 reads 8–16 V;
    • pin 2 shows continuity to GND with the power off;
    • pins 1, 3 and 5 sit near 5 V.
    Earlier hardware revisions used a different AUX pinout.
  • Ready-made 6-wire telephone cables are often reversed (pin 1 at one end is pin 6 at the other). Check continuity end to end, or use a screw-terminal breakout at the mount.
  • Looking into the mount's AUX jack with the latch slot at the bottom, the contacts from left to right are: PDL, data, +12 V, data, GND, DROP. The panel schematic numbers these 1–6 left to right. This page numbers them from the plug side, so DROP is plug pin 1.
  • Inside the mount the two data contacts are the same wire (TX and RX joined), so the bus really is single-wire.

How it works. The Pi's TX (pin 8) and its Drop output (GPIO17, pin 11) each drive one open-drain buffer channel: a low input pulls the mount's line low, and a high input lets it float up to 5 V through the motor board's own 10 kΩ pull-ups (MC001 R3 on RXTX, R4 on DROP). Nothing at 5 V ever reaches a Pi pin, the Pi never pushes the bus high, and the cable needs no 5 V from the Pi. R1/R2 hold the buffer inputs high while the Pi boots, so the mount sees an idle bus.

The mount's data back to the Pi passes through the R5/R6 divider, 47k over 100k: 5 V × 100/147 ≈ 3.4 V at most, about 3.2 V in practice. The values are high on purpose. The divider loads the 10 kΩ pull-up, and the PIC16F886's serial input needs at least 0.8 × 5 V = 4.0 V to read a 1. A 10k/20k divider would pull the idle bus down to about 3.75 V, too low for the motor board. With 47k/100k the bus stays at about 4.7 V.

Grey pads 1, 3, 5 and 6 are already used by the BNO055 IMU; leave them alone. Pin 13 (GPIO27) is only for the pin-scan step and isn't part of the finished cable. The jumper colors are suggestions; the board-to-plug colors depend on your flat cable, so identify each conductor by continuity.

Breadboard layout

Plate 3 of 4

Columns are numbered 1–30 and rows a–e and f–j, as printed on most half-size breadboards. The SN74LVC2G07 adapter straddles the centre gap at columns 10–12: pins 1 (1A), 2 (GND) and 3 (2A) are in row f, and pins 6 (1Y), 5 (VCC) and 4 (2Y) are in row e. Check your adapter's silkscreen, because some adapters number the pins differently. The top and bottom rails are joined at the right-hand end.

Dashed parts are for stage 1 only (proving the interface without the mount): two 10 kΩ resistors from a 5 V point (Pi pin 2) to the bus and Drop columns stand in for the motor board's own pull-ups. Remove them, and the pin 2 wire, before connecting the mount. The mount's 12 V pin is never brought to the breadboard.
